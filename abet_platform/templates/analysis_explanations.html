Observed and fitted values

An observed point is the mean of the records for its term{% if analysis.campus_trends %} and campus{% endif %}. A fitted value is the value on the straight trend line; it is a model summary, not another assessment result.

{% if analysis.campus_trends %}Each campus is fitted separately using at least three observed terms. Target marks use that campus’s mean configured target in each term.{% else %}The line is fitted to selected term means using the configured term order; at least two terms are needed.{% endif %} Observed term means receive equal weight in the fit.

β, R², and p

β (beta) is the slope: the fitted change in percentage points (pp) per {% if analysis.campus_trends %}position on the ordered selected-term axis. It is not necessarily a change per semester or year{% else %}configured term unit{% endif %}. Positive means rising; negative means falling. For example, β = −0.58 means a fitted decrease of 0.58 percentage points for each step on that axis.

R² is the fraction of variation among term means accounted for by the fitted straight line. It is not the percentage of students meeting target or the probability that the model is correct.

p tests a zero-slope model under the regression assumptions. A small p-value is evidence against that model, not proof that an intervention worked. At least three observed terms and variation among their means are required to report a slope p-value. Repeated assessments, changing course mix, and few terms limit interpretation. Trend method.

Statistical interpretation

Dunn’s test

Dunn’s test compares each pair of Bloom levels using ranks calculated across all selected levels. Equal values receive equal average ranks, and the calculation corrects for these ties. Holm adjustment accounts for testing several pairs at once. Use the adjusted p-value, not the raw p-value, when deciding which pairs warrant follow-up.

The figure shows the smallest adjusted p-value and the number of pairs below 0.05; the table gives every pair. All comparisons are shown for transparency, even if the overall Kruskal–Wallis result is not significant. “Not detected” does not mean the groups are equivalent. Counts are assessment records, and repeated courses or students can violate independence. Dunn method reference.

Kruskal–Wallis

H measures separation among the groups’ ranked attainment values. It is not a percentage. df is the number of included groups minus one. The test asks whether the groups have the same underlying distribution; it does not identify which pairs differ.

p is the probability of a test statistic at least this extreme if the null model and test assumptions hold. It is not the probability that the groups are equal or that an ABET requirement is met.

Independent observations and adequate group sizes are required; groups with fewer than five measures warrant caution. These records may repeat courses or students. Treat the result as exploratory unless those assumptions are checked. Test method.

Cliff’s delta

Delta (Δ) compares every Analyze-level record with every nonblank, non-Analyze record. It is the fraction of pairs where Analyze is higher minus the fraction where it is lower. Ties contribute zero.

Δ ranges from −1 to +1. Positive values favor Analyze; negative values favor the other levels. Zero means balanced pairwise ordering, not identical distributions. The magnitude label summarizes effect size; it is not an attainment percentage or a test of statistical significance.

Pairwise effect sizes

The table also gives Cliff’s Δ for each first/second pair. Positive means the first listed level tends to be higher; negative means the second tends to be higher. This differs from the figure’s single Analyze-versus-all-other-levels comparison.

Magnitude labels use absolute Δ: negligible below 0.147, small below 0.33, medium below 0.474, and large otherwise. These are descriptive conventions, not ABET targets. A large effect with a large adjusted p-value may reflect limited evidence; a small effect with a small p-value need not be educationally important.
